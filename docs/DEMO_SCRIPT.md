# PawPath Proof-of-Concept Demo Script

## Purpose

This script is designed to demonstrate the reason PawPath exists in approximately two to three minutes. It should be usable for an informal conversation, screen recording, product review, or early user-feedback session.

## Core message

> Google Maps can show nearby veterinarians. PawPath helps a traveler prepare a primary care option and backup before the trip, then reach the right information quickly if something happens.

## Demonstration scenario

Use a consistent story:

> We are taking our dog, Scout, on a camping trip in an unfamiliar area. We want to know where emergency veterinary care is before we leave—not after something has already happened.

A curated demo destination should be used until live data is complete enough to guarantee a consistent walkthrough.

## Two-minute walkthrough

### 1. Introduce the problem — 15 seconds

“Most of us could search Google Maps for a veterinarian, but while traveling that still leaves us figuring out which clinic handles emergencies, whether it is appropriate, what our backup is, and where all of that information will be when we are under pressure. PawPath is designed around that missing care-planning step.”

### 2. Choose Plan a Trip — 15 seconds

Select **Plan a Trip**.

Explain:

“PawPath begins with the trip destination rather than waiting until an emergency occurs.”

Enter the demonstration campground or destination.

### 3. Review prioritized care — 25 seconds

Show the categorized results and confidence indicators.

Explain:

“The results are organized around the type of care. PawPath distinguishes source-listed information from inferred emergency capability and clearly shows what still needs to be confirmed.”

Open one facility’s detail panel.

### 4. Create the care plan — 30 seconds

Choose one facility as **Primary Emergency Care** and another as **Backup**.

Optionally select a routine-care clinic.

Enter:

- Trip name
- Pet name: Scout
- Owner phone
- Short note such as “Daily medication in travel bag”

Save the plan.

Explain:

“The outcome is not simply a map search. It is a saved plan for this trip.”

### 5. Show the saved plan — 20 seconds

Point out:

- Destination
- Primary facility
- Backup facility
- Call actions
- Directions actions
- Confidence and confirmation reminders

Refresh the page if appropriate to demonstrate that the plan remains saved locally.

### 6. Open Emergency Mode — 25 seconds

Select **Open Emergency Mode**.

Explain:

“If something happens during the trip, PawPath removes the planning interface and presents the most important next actions: call the primary facility, open directions, see the backup, and access the pet’s essential information.”

Show the large Call and Directions actions.

### 7. Show the emergency card — 20 seconds

Open the printable emergency card.

Explain:

“The plan can also be printed or saved as a PDF before the trip, which is useful when cellular service may be unreliable or another person is caring for the pet.”

### 8. Close with the distinction — 10 seconds

“Google Maps helps find businesses. PawPath helps travelers make and carry a pet-care plan.”

## Thirty-second elevator demo

For a very short demonstration:

1. Open a saved demo trip.
2. Show the primary and backup facilities.
3. Open Emergency Mode.
4. Show the Call, Directions, and Backup actions.
5. Close with: “PawPath turns a general map search into a trip-specific pet-care plan.”

## Questions to ask demo viewers

After the demonstration, ask:

1. In your own words, what is PawPath for?
2. How is it different from searching Google Maps?
3. Which part of the experience felt most useful?
4. What information would you need before trusting a facility selection?
5. Would you create a care plan before a trip with your pet?
6. Would you print, save, or share the emergency card?
7. What would prevent you from using this?
8. What is missing from the emergency workflow?

## Success signal

The most important early validation is not whether viewers like the colors or map. It is whether they independently describe PawPath as some version of:

- A pet-travel care plan
- Emergency preparedness for traveling with pets
- A way to choose and save veterinary options before a trip
- A simplified emergency workflow with a backup

If viewers describe it only as “a map of veterinarians,” the product story or POC remains incomplete.

## Demonstration cautions

- Clearly label curated demo records.
- Do not state that a facility is open or available unless the information is reliable and current.
- Do not claim PawPath medically determines where a pet should go.
- Reinforce that users should call ahead.
- Explain that the current public-data implementation is a POC, not yet a complete national veterinary database.
